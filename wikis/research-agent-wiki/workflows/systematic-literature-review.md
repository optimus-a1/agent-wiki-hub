# Systematic Literature Review

## Purpose
Provide a stable workflow for structured literature review without asserting current coverage.

## When to use
Use when organizing research questions, inclusion criteria, evidence tables, and synthesis.

## Stable knowledge points
- KP-01: A review question should define population, phenomenon, method, task, or outcome as appropriate.
- KP-02: Search strategy should be documented so another reviewer can inspect coverage.
- KP-03: Inclusion and exclusion criteria reduce cherry-picking.
- KP-04: Screening should separate title or abstract review from full-text review when practical.
- KP-05: Extraction tables make claims, methods, datasets, metrics, and limitations comparable.
- KP-06: Quality appraisal helps prevent weak evidence from dominating conclusions.
- KP-07: Synthesis should group studies by method, claim type, and evidence strength.
- KP-08: A review should state gaps and uncertainty rather than forcing a single conclusion.

## Core rules
- Do not claim the review is exhaustive unless the search protocol supports it.
- Do not invent missing papers or citations.
- Keep time-sensitive coverage claims out unless sourced.
- Preserve conflicting evidence.

## Workflow
1. Define the research question and review scope.
2. Specify search terms, source classes, and screening criteria.
3. Screen candidate sources and record exclusion reasons.
4. Extract structured evidence from included sources.
5. Synthesize findings, limitations, conflicts, and gaps.

## Edge cases
- Search terms can miss synonyms or adjacent fields.
- Highly cited work can still be methodologically weak.
- Newer work is not automatically better than older work.

## Validation checks
- Scope and criteria are explicit.
- Evidence table fields are consistent.
- Conflicting findings are retained.
- No unsourced current coverage claim is made.

## Source notes
Stable review workflow only. No current literature inventory, search result count, or database coverage is included.
