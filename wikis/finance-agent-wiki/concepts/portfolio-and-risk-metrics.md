# Portfolio And Risk Metrics

## Purpose
Define stable portfolio and risk measurement concepts for educational research, simulation, and review.

## When to use
Use when an agent needs to describe portfolio exposure, concentration, drawdown, leverage, liquidity, or risk-adjusted performance without making personalized investment recommendations.

## Stable knowledge points
- KP-01: Portfolio risk is a property of combined positions, correlations, constraints, and behavior under stress, not a property of isolated assets only.
- KP-02: Exposure describes what the portfolio is sensitive to; position size describes how much capital is assigned.
- KP-03: Gross exposure measures total absolute exposure, while net exposure measures directional offset after long and short positions are combined.
- KP-04: Drawdown measures decline from a prior equity peak and should be reviewed by depth, duration, and recovery behavior.
- KP-05: Volatility describes dispersion of returns; it does not by itself describe liquidity, tail loss, or execution risk.
- KP-06: Correlation can reduce or amplify portfolio risk, but historical correlation may change during stress.
- KP-07: Liquidity risk appears when a position cannot be reduced at expected cost, size, or speed.
- KP-08: Risk-adjusted performance must be interpreted with sample quality, fees, slippage, leverage, and capacity limits.

## Core rules
- Do not turn metrics into personal advice or trade instructions.
- Separate realized risk from modeled risk.
- State assumptions whenever comparing strategies.
- Prefer ranges, scenarios, and limitations over single-number certainty.

## Workflow
1. Identify positions, instruments, notional exposure, cash, leverage, and constraints.
2. Group exposures by asset class, currency, sector, factor, counterparty, or strategy theme.
3. Review concentration, volatility, drawdown, liquidity, and stress sensitivity.
4. Compare risk metrics against stated research objectives and simulated capital limits.
5. Document uncertainty, missing data, and required human confirmation.

## Edge cases
- Hedged books can still be exposed to basis risk, funding risk, or liquidity gaps.
- A low-volatility strategy can carry hidden tail risk.
- High turnover can make a strategy dependent on execution assumptions.

## Validation checks
- Metrics are labeled as educational or simulated.
- No individualized recommendation is produced.
- Data limits and assumptions are visible.
- Human confirmation is required before real capital decisions.

## Source notes
Stable risk measurement principles only. No current market data, prices, platform rules, or investment advice are included.
